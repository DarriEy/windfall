# Literature positioning & novelty (scan 2026-06)

## Verdict
Novel in (a) the dual-purpose / wake-as-community-shelter FRAMING and (b) the
terrain-selectivity MECHANISM (confined fjord leaks the shelter over its
ridges; open coast holds it). Not novel in method (WRF+Fitch) or in the fact
that wakes persist downstream (well documented). One adjacent precedent
(Jacobson 2014) must be cited and distinguished.

## Defensible novelty statement (keep the "to our knowledge" hedge)
"Wind-farm wakes are universally treated as a generation loss to be minimized,
and large arrays have been proposed only to weaken tropical cyclones (Jacobson
et al. 2014); we are, to our knowledge, the first to evaluate deliberately
siting a utility-scale wind farm as an everyday windbreak for a specific
community, and to show the generation- and shelter-optimal configurations are
antagonistic and terrain-dependent — confined fjord terrain being a *worse*
shelter site than open coast, contrary to intuition and reduced-order
prediction (the wake diverts up and over the bounding ridges)."

## The precedent to distinguish (a reviewer WILL raise it)
- **Jacobson, Archer & Bauer (2014), "Taming hurricanes with arrays of offshore
  wind turbines," Nature Climate Change 4, 195-200.** Offshore farms as
  protective public good — but EXTREME tropical cyclones via wind->wave->
  friction feedback. DISTINGUISH: (i) everyday/annual-mean wind vs extreme;
  (ii) terrain-driven flow vs cyclonic; (iii) our cut-out/feathering result
  (turbines feather >25 m/s) is the ANTI-Jacobson finding — ordinary farms
  cannot shelter the worst storms, so the realizable benefit is the everyday
  wind. Frame our cut-out result as a direct, contributing counterpoint.
